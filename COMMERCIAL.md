If you can comply with the AGPL, you do not need a commercial license.

corresponding source to users who interact with a modified network

## When a commercial license is the right tool

A separate, paid copyright grant is available if you **cannot** comply
with the AGPL. Typical cases:

- You want to ship this software (or a modified version) inside a
  proprietary product and not provide AGPL source to your users.
- You want to run a modified instance as a network service without
  offering that modified source to the service’s users.
- Your counsel has forbidden AGPL (or “or later”) in your environment,
  but you still want to use this codebase.

The commercial grant is a **parallel license from the copyright holder**.
It does not amend, weaken, or replace the AGPL text in `LICENSE`. The
public AGPL copy of the project stays AGPL.

## What it covers (and what it does not)

A commercial license, once executed in writing, typically grants the
right to use, modify, and distribute agreed versions **without** the
AGPL’s copyleft / source-offer obligations.

It does **not**, unless a separate agreement says so:

- include support, hosting, or custom development
- relicense third-party dependencies (FastAPI, Uvicorn, etc. keep their
  own licenses)
- relicense contributions from other people unless those contributors
  assigned or dual-licensed their work

Pricing and contract terms are not published in this repository. They
are agreed in writing.

## How to ask

Open a GitHub issue on
[ldjessee-code/local-asset-management](https://github.com/ldjessee-code/local-asset-management)
with the title **Commercial license** and a short description of the
product or service you need to cover.

Do not send secrets or customer data in the issue.

unless a contributor license agreement is in place. None is in force
yet. If dual-licensing of a combined work becomes blocked by third-party
contributions, a CLA may be introduced later; it will not be retroactive
without consent.
